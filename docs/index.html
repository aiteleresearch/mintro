FAQ

Good questions.

Will this make me money?

Probably not much in dollars today. A laptop's mining earns cents to a few dollars a month, and electricity can cost more. The idea is to collect small amounts of several early coins in case one becomes valuable. Most won't. Not financial advice.

Does mining more coins at once earn more?

No. Your laptop has a fixed speed, and the basket spreads it across several bets. It changes what you hold, not how much compute you have.

Why does Windows Security flag it?

Every antivirus flags crypto-mining engines, including the open-source ones MINTRO uses. MINTRO downloads them from their official releases and checks their fingerprints. When Windows Security blocks one, the card explains why and offers to exclude only MINTRO's miner folder, behind Windows' own admin prompt.

Windows says it protected my PC. Is the installer safe?

SmartScreen shows that screen for apps it hasn't yet seen many people install, and MINTRO is new and not code-signed yet. Choose More info, then Run anyway. The installer needs no admin rights: it only writes to your own user folder, and Settings > Apps removes it. Its source code is public on GitHub.

Does MINTRO take a cut?

No. Every share goes to your own wallet address. The engines keep their own small developer fees (XMRig 1%, SRBMiner-Multi about 0.85%, cpuminer-opt none), listed in the app's settings.

Will it slow my laptop down?

In Auto mode it drops to idle priority the moment you use the PC and uses fewer threads while you work. It pauses on battery. Like any sustained load it runs the fans; Eco mode keeps things cool and quiet.

Is everything really free?

Yes: GPU mining, the tax report, alerts, My PCs, all of it. MINTRO is paid for by coin projects that list their coins. Listed and featured coins are always labelled, and you can switch off featured listings' guaranteed share in Settings.

Is it open source?

Yes, under GPL-3.0. Read the code, build it yourself, or contribute on GitHub.
