How to mine crypto on a laptop.

Yes, an ordinary Windows laptop can mine cryptocurrency. It won't make you rich, and on big coins it barely covers the electricity. Here's what it really earns, what it costs, which coins a processor can mine, and how to start in a few minutes.

  1. 01The short answer

    Can you mine crypto on a laptop?

    Yes, but not Bitcoin. Bitcoin is mined by ASICs, machines built for nothing else; a laptop would earn practically zero there. Other coins chose proof-of-work algorithms that run best on an ordinary processor. Monero's RandomX is the best known; GhostRider, yespower and others work the same way. On those coins your laptop competes on fair terms with other people's PCs.

    Mining means your processor repeatedly hashes candidate blocks; whoever finds a valid one gets the block reward. A laptop alone would wait months for a block, so miners join a pool that shares each reward in proportion to the work everyone did. That's why small amounts arrive steadily instead of all at once, or never.

  2. 02Earnings

    What a laptop really earns

    Your share of a coin's new supply is your speed divided by the whole network's speed. An example at the time of writing:

    • A typical laptop does about 2,000 hashes a second on RandomX.
    • Monero's whole network does about 5 billion, so the laptop's share is roughly 1 in 2.5 million.
    • Monero issues about 432 XMR a day (0.6 per block, one block every two minutes).
    • So the laptop mines about 0.00017 XMR a day: around 9 US cents at $550 per XMR, before electricity.

    A laptop mining flat out draws perhaps 20 to 30 extra watts: about 0.6 kWh a day, or around 9 cents at $0.15 per kWh. On a big, established coin, a laptop roughly breaks even.

    Small, young coins are different. With only a handful of miners, the same laptop can mine a real slice of what's new: sometimes thousands of coins instead of fractions of one. Most of those coins never gain much value, but a few do. That's the idea behind MINTRO's basket: spread the same speed across several early coins, like holding several lottery tickets, rather than chasing pennies on one big coin. Treat it as a bet, never as an income.

  3. 03Coins

    Which coins a laptop can mine

    Look for coins whose algorithm favours CPUs:

    • RandomX: Monero, Zephyr, Salvium and several newer coins.
    • GhostRider: Raptoreum and coins that copied it.
    • yespower, yescrypt, power2b, MinotaurX: smaller coins built to stay CPU-mineable.

    The coins page lists every coin MINTRO can mine today, with its algorithm, the engine that mines it and whether it trades anywhere yet. If your laptop has a discrete graphics card, GPU coins such as Firo become possible too; on most laptops the heat isn't worth it.

  4. 04What you need

    What you need

    • A Windows 10 or 11 laptop with a 64-bit processor. Anything from the last decade works; more cores and a bigger cache mean more speed.
    • About 2.5 GB of free memory for RandomX coins. With less, RandomX's light mode works at a fraction of the speed; other algorithms need far less.
    • A wallet address for each coin, where the pool sends your coins. You create it with the coin's official wallet and keep its seed words safe: whoever has them can spend the coins.
    • Mining software and a pool. Either set up an engine such as XMRig yourself, or let an app such as MINTRO choose the coins, pools and settings for you.
  5. 05Pools

    Mining pools and payouts

    A pool combines thousands of miners and pays each one their share of every block the pool finds. Each pool sets a minimum payout per coin: your coins collect on the pool until you pass it, then the pool sends them to your wallet. If your laptop is slow on a coin with a high minimum, your coins can sit on the pool for months. MINTRO only mines a coin if your PC will reach its payout within about a week.

    Pools also charge a small fee (often around 1%), and each mining engine may keep a small developer fee. Check both before you start.

  6. 06Your laptop

    Is mining safe for your laptop?

    Mining keeps the processor busy for hours, so it runs warm and the fans spin. Modern processors slow themselves down before they overheat, so they protect themselves, but heat is still what wears a laptop over time. Keep it sensible:

    • Keep the vents clear: a desk, not a bed or a sofa.
    • Don't mine on battery. It drains the battery and wears it faster.
    • Use fewer threads while you work, or a cooler mode such as MINTRO's Eco.
    • If the laptop is uncomfortably hot to touch, stop and let it rest.

    MINTRO's Auto mode does this for you: it drops to idle priority and fewer threads the moment you touch the keyboard, runs fully when the laptop is idle, and pauses on battery.

  7. 07Antivirus

    Why antivirus flags mining software

    Criminals hide miners on other people's computers, so every antivirus, including Windows Security, flags mining engines by design, even the open-source ones downloaded from their official releases. That's a policy, not a sign your copy is infected. Allow only the miner's own folder, never turn your antivirus off, and only use engines from their official releases. MINTRO explains a block on the card and offers to allow just its miner folder, behind Windows' own admin prompt.

  9. 09By hand

    Or do it by hand

    If you'd rather run the engine yourself, the classic route for a RandomX coin is XMRig:

    1. Create a wallet with the coin's official wallet and note its receive address.
    2. Download XMRig from its official GitHub releases and check the published checksum.
    3. Pick a pool for the coin and note its stratum address and port.
    4. Run xmrig -o POOL:PORT -u YOUR_ADDRESS -p x and watch the hashrate.

    That's one coin on one pool, with your antivirus, memory settings and payouts to manage yourself. It's a good way to learn how mining works.

  10. 10Tax and law

    Tax and the law

    Mining is legal in most countries, but not everywhere, and many tax authorities treat mined coins as income at their value on the day you receive them. Rules differ and change, so check yours. MINTRO can export a tax report: a CSV of everything mined per day and coin, with its dollar value at the time.

    The bottom line: mining on a laptop is a cheap way to hold a little of several early coins and to learn how crypto works. It isn't an income, most early coins stay worthless, and nothing here is financial advice.
